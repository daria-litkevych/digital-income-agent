# Competitor and customer-signal research

**Research date:** 12 November 2026  
**Scope:** Public competitor and substitute scan for the two product hypotheses. Prices are observed public prices and should be rechecked before commercial decisions.

## Executive summary

- **Small Café Manager Starter Kit:** Generic paid checklist templates exist at low one-off prices, but the more important finding is that free alternatives already exist. Brewspace advertises a 74-task specialty-coffee operations checklist at no charge; AssignRight lists 10 free coffee-shop checklists. A paid generic checklist pack is therefore a weak proposition.
- **ShiftBrief AI:** There are direct handover/logbook products and broader hospitality suites. Official price examples include ShiftForce from $50/location/month, Shifo at €32/month excl. VAT, Venheim with a free entry tier and €39.90/site/month Pro, and Rotahr at €49/month incl. VAT. These products bundle more than handover alone.
- **Customer pain signals:** Public worker discussions describe chaotic group texts, missed shift swaps/tasks and handover gaps, but also notification overload, app fatigue and micromanagement concerns.
- **No willingness-to-pay conclusion is justified.** Public product pages establish supply and price anchors, not sales, retention or unmet demand.

### Early interpretation

The category is crowded at the low end and includes free, specialty-coffee-specific resources. A paid pack cannot win simply by collecting opening/closing checklists. A stronger hypothesis would solve a costly recurring job: reduce milk/bean/pastry waste, improve espresso consistency, make barista onboarding faster, or help a manager run a reliable weekly routine. It must be tested against the tools owners already use and the free resources they can access.

### Early interpretation

ShiftBrief AI would enter an established category. A narrow wedge could be *handover-first, extremely low-friction*: voice or short text becomes a structured handover, urgent items, owner and due time — without replacing scheduling, payroll or the whole restaurant stack. But the product must prove that teams want a separate tool, not another app. Notification fatigue and adoption by every shift member are critical risks.

## Customer pain signals (anecdotal)

1. A Serverlife discussion describes paper schedules and chaotic group texts leading to missed shift swaps and side-work assignments. Replies mention read receipts as useful, but also app/interface friction and reluctance to pay for apps in very small teams. [Discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/Serverlife/comments/1qau5mp/restaurant_workers_what_apps_does_your_job_use/)
2. A KitchenConfidential discussion describes apps used for shift notes, prep and repairs, alongside complaints about overuse, micromanagement and excessive messages. [Discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/KitchenConfidential/comments/1s1w644/does_everyone_use_apps_now_to_communicate/)

These are self-selected anecdotes, not a representative survey. Competitor free resources and pricing are not proof that customers use or pay for the products.

## Preliminary comparison

| Dimension | Café Starter Kit | ShiftBrief AI |
|---|---|---|
| Competition | Cheap paid templates plus free specialist checklists | Established handover products and broader suites |
| Price pressure | Severe for generic downloadable checklists | Monthly price anchors from free to €49+ |
| Build complexity | Low | Medium/high for multi-user, voice and history |
| Automation potential | High after creation | High, but hosting, privacy and support costs remain |
| Main risk | No reason to pay for generic content | Another app to adopt; feature competition and support |
| Cheapest validation | Show a workflow sample after observing current routines | Prototype one handover and test with shift leads |

## Recommended next validation

Do not build a full SaaS or generic template pack yet.

1. **Specialty coffee:** Speak with 5–8 independent café owners/managers. Ask them to show current opening/closing, stock/waste, quality-control and onboarding routines. Find one recurring problem with measurable time, waste, errors or training cost. Only then test whether they would pay for a specific solution.
2. **Shift handover:** Speak with 5–8 shift leads. Ask how handovers happen now, which information gets lost, what happens when it is missed, what tools they already use, and what they dislike about current apps. Test a paper/WhatsApp-compatible prototype before building a standalone app.

Do not pitch before understanding the current workflow. Record exact quotes, frequency, consequences, workaround and existing spend.

## Evidence limitations

- Etsy marketplace pages are category/search results, not full product audits; sale prices and review counts need product-level checks.
- Free checklist pages establish availability of substitutes, not adoption or customer satisfaction.
- Vendor prices can change and are not directly comparable across currencies, tax treatment, team size and feature scope.
- Front Desk and HospiX prices in this scan are earlier search observations and should be rechecked before a pricing decision.
- Public worker discussions are self-selected and anecdotal.
- No market-size estimate, sales-volume estimate or willingness-to-pay finding is supported by this scan.
