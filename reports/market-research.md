# Initial competitor and customer-signal research

**Research date:** 12 November 2026  
**Scope:** Public web discovery for the two current product hypotheses. Prices are observed page/search-result prices, not audited quotes; recheck before decisions.

## Executive summary

- **Small Café Manager Starter Kit:** generic coffee-shop checklists and SOP packs already exist on Etsy at very low one-off prices. This is evidence of supply and category familiarity, not proof of sales or demand. Differentiation must be more specific than another generic checklist.
- **ShiftBrief AI:** there are direct handover/logbook products and broader hospitality platforms that bundle tasks, chat, scheduling and checklists. Entry-level software ranges from free to roughly €49/month in the examples found, with broader platforms costing more.
- **Customer pain signals:** public worker discussions describe chaotic group texts, missed shift swaps/tasks and handover gaps, but also notification overload, app fatigue and micromanagement concerns.
- **No willingness-to-pay conclusion is justified yet.**

### Early interpretation

The generic template market appears crowded and price-competitive. A stronger angle may be a tightly curated pack for independent specialty coffee shops: opening/closing, espresso machine cleaning, dial-in and waste logs, milk/alternative milk handling, handover, stock rotation and barista onboarding — all immediately usable without configuring a complex workspace. This is a product hypothesis, not validated demand.

### Early interpretation

A narrow wedge could be *handover-first, extremely low-friction*: voice or short text input becomes a structured handover, urgent items, owner and due time — without replacing scheduling, payroll or the whole restaurant management stack. This positioning still needs customer validation.

## Customer pain signals (anecdotal)

1. A Serverlife discussion describes paper schedules and chaotic group texts leading to missed shift swaps and side-work assignments. Replies mention read receipts as useful, but also app/interface friction and reluctance to pay for apps in very small teams. [Discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/Serverlife/comments/1qau5mp/restaurant_workers_what_apps_does_your_job_use/)
2. A KitchenConfidential discussion describes apps used for shift notes, prep and repairs, alongside complaints about overuse, micromanagement and excessive messages. [Discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/KitchenConfidential/comments/1s1w644/does_everyone_use_apps_now_to_communicate/)

These are qualitative anecdotes, not a representative survey.

## Preliminary comparison

| Dimension | Café Starter Kit | ShiftBrief AI |
|---|---|---|
| Competition | Many cheap templates | Established SaaS and broad operations suites |
| Price pressure | Very high on generic downloads | Subscription price anchors visible |
| Build complexity | Low | Medium/high if real-time multi-user app |
| Automation potential | High after creation | High, but hosting/support and AI costs possible |
| Key risk | Hard to stand out and acquire buyers | Feature competition, integrations, privacy and ongoing support |
| Cheapest validation | Show a sample pack to café owners/managers | Prototype handover flow and test with shift leads |

## Recommended next validation

Do not build a full SaaS yet. Conduct 5–8 short discovery conversations with independent specialty coffee shop owners/managers and 5–8 with horeca shift leads. Ask them to demonstrate their current process before showing a solution. Record current tools, frequency of failures, consequences, workarounds and whether they already pay to solve the problem.

## Evidence limitations

- Marketplace pages are search/category pages, not full product audits.
- Listed prices may be promotional, regional or subject to change.
- Some ShiftBrief alternatives are substitutes rather than direct competitors.
- Public worker discussions are self-selected and anecdotal.
- Search snippets do not establish sales volume, retention, customer satisfaction or willingness to pay.
